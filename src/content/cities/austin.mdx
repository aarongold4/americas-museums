Austin's museum scene punches above its weight for a city its size, in part because UT Austin runs three of the heaviest hitters within a few blocks of each other — the Blanton, the Ransom Center, and the LBJ Library — and in part because the state capital adds a fourth: the Bullock. You can see those four on foot in a long day, and most of them are either free or close to it.

The rest of the list fills in what the big four miss: contemporary art at the Contemporary Austin, Mexican and Latino art at Mexic-Arte, East Austin's Black history at the Carver, a quirky 19th-century sculptor's limestone studio at the Elisabet Ney, and an open-air sculpture garden below Zilker Park at the UMLAUF. Six of the twelve are free, which is unusually generous for a city with no wealth-tax-funded museum endowment.

The Blanton bought itself a national headline when it reopened a renovated campus and set an attendance record in 2024 — go first if you only have one morning. If you have kids under ten, Thinkery is the one. And whatever you do, make time for Ellsworth Kelly's Austin on the Blanton grounds; it's one of the only buildings the artist designed, and it's effectively a last testament.
